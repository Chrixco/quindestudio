RepoSync privacy policy

What RepoSync does with your data: it stays on your Mac and your own devices.

Last updated 25 November 2026.

The short version

RepoSync doesn't collect, share or sell any personal data, and sends nothing to Quinde Studio. There are no accounts, no ads, no tracking, no analytics and no third-party SDKs. It never asks for or stores your git or GitHub passwords or tokens, and it never sends the contents of your changes anywhere except to your own git remotes when it pushes.

Git and GitHub

RepoSync runs the git already on your Mac, with your own git setup, to commit, fetch and push to the remotes you've configured. Signing in to those remotes is handled by your git credential helper or SSH keys, as it would be in Terminal.

To decide whether a change is big or risky, RepoSync looks at file names, sizes and line counts on your Mac. If the GitHub command line tool is installed, it asks GitHub whether a repository is public or private, using your existing gh login, and remembers the answer for a day.

What RepoSync stores on your Mac

  • Settings, such as the folders and repositories to watch, the schedule and your limits, in RepoSync's preferences.
  • A fingerprint of changes you skipped, so it doesn't ask about the same change twice. It's a hash of the change summary, not the change itself.
  • A log at ~/Library/Logs/RepoSync.log with repository names, push and approval events, the names of files that look like secrets, and the name and network address of any paired iPhone or iPad. You can delete it at any time.
  • Paired devices in your Mac's keychain. A device that hasn't connected for 30 days is removed.

Claude usage

The Claude usage panel reads the usage and session files that Claude Code already saves on your Mac, in ~/.claude. RepoSync never signs in to your Claude account and makes no calls to Anthropic. If you turn on the Claude Code hook, RepoSync adds it to ~/.claude/settings.json, saves a backup of that file first, and installs a small script that records your Claude sessions and budgets in ~/.claude on your Mac.

The usage dashboard is a local page made by a separate script. If that page uses web fonts, your Mac may load them from Google Fonts when you open it, and Google sees that request.

Your iPhone and iPad

iPhone and iPad access is off until you turn it on. When you pair a device, your Mac sends it the information it shows directly over your local network or your Tailscale network, encrypted for that device alone. Nothing goes through our servers, because there aren't any. That information includes your repositories and their changes, your Claude sessions and usage, the open Chrome tabs, your Mac's name, battery and network details used to wake it, and pictures of the one window you choose to watch.

The iPhone and iPad app uses the camera only to scan the pairing code, and Face ID to stop others from controlling your Mac. Its pairing key is kept in the device's keychain, and its widget keeps a short summary in the app's own storage.

Permissions

RepoSync asks macOS for permissions only when you use the feature that needs them: Automation and Accessibility to type into Terminal, iTerm2 or Chrome for you; Screen Recording to show a window on your iPhone or iPad; Local Network to accept your paired devices; Notifications for push and approval alerts; and Login Items if you want it to start with your Mac. You can turn any of them off in System Settings > Privacy & Security.

Email

If you email us, we use your address only to reply, and we delete the conversation if you ask.

Children

Because RepoSync doesn't collect personal information, it doesn't collect it from children.

Your rights

We hold no personal data about you from RepoSync, so there is nothing to access or delete. For emails you've sent us, write to privacy@quindestudio.com and we'll help.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date above and say what changed.

Contact

Quinde Studio, privacy@quindestudio.com. For how this website handles data, see the studio privacy policy.
