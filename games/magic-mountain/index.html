Golden Tickets wait at the viewpoints

Ten tiles carry a camera icon. End your move on one with the Camera, pay one energy, and you’re the first to photograph it: draw a Golden Ticket. Arrive later and the view still pays three energy, so a detour is always worth it.

Tickets are also insurance. Lava may be placed anywhere, even beside the Summit, and if it closes every route the climber holding the most Golden Tickets wins.

Seven more in the deck: Sponsor’s Gift, Sherpa’s Blessing, Weather Charm, Cartographer’s Map, Hidden Energy Cache, Expedition Backpack, Guardian Spirit. One copy of each, and only the first photographer of a viewpoint draws one.

One round is one day on the mountain

Each player takes one full turn. The weather changes for everyone at once, so bad luck reads as weather, never as a personal grudge.

  1. Reveal the Day card

    Clear skies, a blizzard, a spring thaw. The card changes movement costs for the whole round.

  2. Refill your energy

    At the start of your turn, your cards refill you: 4 from your Base Camp, plus each provision in play. Nothing carries over.

  3. Spend it

    Buy terrain, gear or food, photograph a viewpoint and climb as far as your energy and gear allow. In a team game you may also trade with your partner.

  4. Pass to the left

    Unused energy is lost. The next climber faces the same sky.

First you build an engine, then you climb

Everyone starts with a Base Camp worth 4 Energy every turn, and nothing else: no boots, no rope, no tent. The only way to grow is food. Rations give a burst this turn; provisions stay in front of you and pay out every turn for the rest of the game, up to three at a time.

A climber living on four energy takes one step at Elevation 1. With three provisions in play, they can reach ten or more, enough for two steps at Elevation 3 or one on the ice.

Eight pieces of gear, one per turn

Climbers start with nothing and buy one piece per turn. The box holds four of every piece, one per player, so nothing can be bought out from under you, and each price sits below the income you’ll have when the mountain first asks for it. Rivals never hand over their rope; only a teammate will.

    For publishers

    Magic Mountain: Reach for the Peak: a family-weight expedition game with a physical board that grows on the table.

    • Table presence. Snow, rock, water and lava are sculpted in relief. Prototyped in 3D print, designed for injection moulding.
    • Self-explaining components. Colour-coded, numbered frames and gear icons on every tile. New players read the mountain instead of the rulebook.
    • A readable economy. Energy never accumulates, which gives every game a clear arc: build income, then climb.
    • A different board every game. The frames go down in any shape, with no connection rules, and the tiles are drawn blind. No two expeditions climb the same mountain.
    • Scalable. One set of frames plays as a 30-minute family climb or a full expedition.
    • Room to grow. New terrain sets, Day cards and themed mountains plug into the same frame system.
